# Source-selection coverage corrections

Approved exercise instructions can describe the Crown as bearing the burden of
proving or establishing an approved threshold. The threshold must still match the
current approved model or controlling instruction. Reported source statements,
actual filed charges, factual payments, unapproved currencies and neighboring
amounts remain subject to source checks.

Proposed-count references are checked against the single controlling approved
value element by amount and currency. The burden can precede or follow the count
reference, or use passive proof wording. Only the benchmark occurrence is removed
from factual amount checking; another occurrence of the same number still needs
its own support. Quoted, reported and historical words cannot supply the author's
normative scope, and exercise approval does not establish an actual filed count.

Approved proof lists also recognize articles and subject-matter value labels,
including a list ending with "and a subject-matter value exceeding" the approved
amount. A source reporting such a proof list still needs source support; it does
not become a delivered or approved instruction.

A current Crown proof directive can retain its subject across a long advocacy
lead ending in "but must prove" or "and must establish". Sentence, clause and
quotation boundaries govern this scope. A direct statement that an amount is the
approved element's threshold also checks that amount against the unique current
instruction. Neither form establishes an actual charge or exempts adjacent source
payments. An independent checker rejection remains withheld.

Recorded-figure comparisons keep decimal points inside their source clause. A
preceding decimal amount cannot erase the report attribution or named exhibit for
a later comparison. Actual sentence and semicolon boundaries, currency and
inequality checks, and independent factual/proof restrictions still apply.

A value-element statement requiring proof beyond a reasonable doubt can refer to
the approved threshold without claiming a transaction. It still needs one matching
current instruction and currency; source reports, quotations, actual charge claims
and neighboring payments keep their own support requirements.

Generated element and proof assessments preserve the approved term deprivation.
An unqualified depreciation assessment with no supporting source context is
withheld for a checked repair. Genuine asset depreciation and quoted source words
remain unchanged; the checker never silently corrects historical output.
An introductory phrase does not hide an explicit element claim. A later
evidential qualifier does not make a preceding proof requirement conditional;
questions, denials and conditions on the requirement retain their own scope.

Authors and repairs state application source-use restrictions in a separate setup
sentence, using the actual supplied permissions. Transcript introductory pages,
oaths and attributed answers do not establish those permissions. An irrelevant
exhibit/page/paragraph citation must be removed or replaced with genuine support;
factual clauses retain their own citations. Removing a locator never accepts the
replacement automatically: the complete response is checked again against the
original source-use context, and unsupported proof permissions remain failures.

Author repair prompts repeat each editable unit's actual deterministic and
semantic errors beside its source text, including failures of an otherwise
analysis-labelled finding. Joint errors remain available separately. The author
must resolve the rejected clause itself; appending an explanation does not repair
an unchanged unsupported premise. Accepted units remain immutable, the retry
allowance is unchanged, and the complete replacement is freshly checked.

For a quotation sharing a trailing exhibit citation with other quoted fragments,
an omitted source paragraph can trigger a same-draft checker correction. Any
candidate locators come only from the intended source's permitted passages and
matching words. Candidates do not prove the speaker, qualification or meaning,
and are never added as accepted selectors. The checker must explicitly select
and assess the actual passage in a complete fresh finding, or reject the claim.
Numbered transcript lines continue to use the existing quotation matcher.

When a positive checker finding on an unchanged unit removes or replaces earlier
source paragraph selections, TrialLens requires an independent review of the
complete unit against only its current selected paragraphs. The earlier review is
a consistency signal, not proof. Earlier citations are never restored or combined
with current citations to manufacture coverage.

The focused review receives the unchanged unit and its current selected source
paragraphs with their original paragraph identifiers. Other units, full source
bodies, summaries, public submissions, earlier checker explanations and private
preparation are excluded. Its complete finding is checked against that restricted
inventory, then composed with the other actual findings and rechecked against the
original source permissions and whole-response constraints. A mixed claim needing
public-record support can remain unresolved in this restricted review.

Source-selection anchors survive checker failures and author repairs for unchanged
text. They are bound to the permitted source projection; a changed source or
permission scope invalidates the old anchor. Reordering or duplicating the same
selection does not trigger a review. Complete alternative passages can pass an
independent review without retaining an earlier mistaken selection.

A correction round reviews each affected unit at most once, sequentially in
separate selected-only requests. Multiple changed selections share the round;
they do not require one correction round per unit. No unit receives another
unit's text, passages or review. A unit with an empty current selection receives
no source passages. The combined result still undergoes the original full-request
permission, history and whole-response checks.

Every individual HTTP request counts against the shared provider request/time
budget. The configured correction-round limit and transport retries remain
unchanged. A rejected or malformed focus stops the queue with the response
withheld; a transport/budget failure preserves completed checks and the unresolved
claims. Missing fields, unavailable selectors, unresolved coverage and exhausted
rounds remain failures. Correction rounds are not HTTP-request counts.

Judgment consistency is tracked for each complete, valid unit while another
unit's locator is corrected. Global schema errors do not establish comparable
findings. A changed judgment on unchanged text requires an independent recheck
against the actual record; either acceptance or rejection can be confirmed.
Repeated reversal at the correction cap stays withheld. An independent substantive
failure survives a sibling citation correction.

These controls detect selection loss across reviews. They do not independently
prove the semantic completeness of a first, stable model selection. A fresh frozen
browser acceptance run remains necessary before concluding that the real case
completes or reporting a simulated jury decision.
