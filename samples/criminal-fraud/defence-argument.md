# Fictional defence counsel argument

The defence accepts receipt of the deposit and the operating transfer but disputes the Crown's inference of dishonest intent. The customer email says that operating funds may be used temporarily while scheduling the equipment. Its later reservation is ambiguous and does not settle what authorization Morgan understood at the time.

The receipt and banking record prove transactions, not the accused's state of mind. The packet contains no complete cash-flow history, supplier correspondence, or contemporaneous admission of a plan never to deliver. Non-delivery and an unpaid refund are consistent with a failed business transaction. Counsel submits that the Crown has not eliminated the reasonable possibility of authorized temporary use followed by financial failure.

Request acquittal if the required proof is missing. Do not reverse the burden, assert that the customer definitely authorized diversion, or invent favourable documents. This argument is private advocacy, not evidence.
