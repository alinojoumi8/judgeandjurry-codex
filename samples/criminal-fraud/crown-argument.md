# Fictional Crown argument

The Crown relies on the combined chronology, rather than non-delivery alone. The signed receipt describes a $20,000 equipment deposit. The banking record then shows $15,000 transferred to operating expenses before any equipment was delivered. The customer email permits scheduling flexibility but says to keep the order deposit available.

The Crown submits that this sequence supports an inference of knowing diversion. The defence's temporary-use explanation must be tested against the reservation about retaining the deposit. A genuine business difficulty is possible, but the Crown says the contemporaneous documents weaken that explanation. The unresolved question is dishonest intent; the Crown bears the burden of proving the allegation beyond a reasonable doubt.

Use only the three imported sources. Do not treat this argument as evidence, invent admissions, or claim that non-delivery alone establishes guilt.
